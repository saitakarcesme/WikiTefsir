# WikiTefsir visual-source guide

## Editorial boundary

WikiTefsir will not depict prophets or invent their physical appearance. Story pages should use geography, routes, landscapes, manuscripts, inscriptions, objects, architecture, and restrained abstract diagrams. A visual must not be presented as proof of a Quranic event or location when the Quran does not make that identification.

## Preferred reusable collections

1. **The Metropolitan Museum of Art Open Access** — public-domain Quran folios and Islamic manuscripts with stable object metadata and downloadable images. Strong use: article headers about revelation, manuscript history, and textual transmission. Example object: <https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/455067>.
2. **Wikimedia Commons, file-level licensing only** — historic maps of Egypt, Sinai, Arabia, and the eastern Mediterranean. Use only after checking the individual file’s license and attribution requirements. Strong use: geography context; never label a disputed traditional route as Quranically certain. Candidate map: <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:1856_Kiepert_Map_of_the_Sinai_Peninsula,_Egypt_-_Geographicus_-_Sinai-kiepert-1856.jpg>.
3. **Library of Congress Geography and Map Division** — scan records with item-level rights statements. Strong use: neutral regional maps and historical cartography. Candidate Sinai record: <https://www.loc.gov/item/2012592017/>.

## Story-page visual pattern

- Use one quiet, wide contextual visual at most per major story section.
- Place the caption and license next to the image, not on a detached credits page.
- Label historical maps as historical artifacts, not as verified event maps.
- Prefer a custom data diagram for the Quranic reading sequence; prefer archival material for atmosphere and geography.
- Generated imagery, if used later, may cover landscapes, material culture, or abstract light and texture. It must not depict a prophet, Companion, or claimed sacred event as a witnessed scene.

## Required asset record

Every imported visual must record: source page URL, creator, date, collection, license/status, download URL, local filename, crop/edit note, caption, and the article in which it is used.
